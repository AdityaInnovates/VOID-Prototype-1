# Pairing a 1.2B reflex with Qwen3.5-4B

**Recommendation: use a ~1.2B liquid-style model as the reflex brain and `mlx-community/Qwen3.5-4B-4bit` (non-thinking) as the deep brain, and treat both as hypotheses until a 30-minute A/B on your own Mac.** For the reflex, `mlx-community/LFM2-1.2B-4bit` (659 MB) is the safest verified pick, and its newer sibling LFM2.5-1.2B has the best published instruction-following score under 2B; I could not confirm an mlx-community LFM2.5 conversion, so check for one. For the deep brain, Qwen3.5-4B has the only usable emotional-intelligence number among small models (EQ-Bench 3: 22.6 vs 14.7 for the 2B), the smallest KV cache of any 4B candidate, and a measured ~32 tok/s on an M3. Nothing in the evidence directly measures warmth, short-reply discipline or 8 GB behaviour, so every "chat quality" statement below is inference from proxies. Three engineering conclusions are firmer because they come from reading the 3.32.3 source: Qwen3.5 must be run with `enable_thinking: false` passed explicitly; classic draft-model speculative decoding cannot work on Qwen3.5; and prefix prompt caching is the largest latency lever by far. The main risk is memory: the 4B plus a 1.2B plus STT and TTS sits near the ~5-6 GB budget that an 8 GB Mac gives the GPU, so keep a 2B fallback ready.

## What is measured, what is inferred

Most evidence here is indirect, so the labels matter. **Measured on an M3-class chip:** Qwen3.5-4B 4-bit decodes at about **32 tok/s** and prefills at about **330-340 tok/s** on a 16 GB M3 ([oMLX community benchmarks](https://omlx.ai/benchmarks?page=2307)); Qwen3.5-2B 6-bit decodes at **50 tok/s** and prefills at **651 tok/s** with a 3.0 GB peak at 4k context on a 24 GB M3 ([oMLX entry](https://omlx.ai/benchmarks/o3bhfwom)). These rows are crowd-submitted and unverified, and none came from an 8 GB machine. **Read from source (not run):** everything about mlx-swift-lm 3.32.3 APIs and architecture support, which was checked at the exact tag. **Inferred:** all other speeds (scaled from memory bandwidth), the 8 GB headroom, the quality of any model's chat voice, and every latency saving from caching. Decode speed follows a rule of thumb of 0.7-0.75 x 100 GB/s divided by weight bytes; the measured 4B number is about 75% of its 43 tok/s ceiling ([bestllmfor](https://bestllmfor.com/guide/llm-macbook-air-m3/)), which makes the scaling credible but not proven for other families.

## Deep brain: why Qwen3.5-4B, and what could overturn it

Quality evidence is thin, but it points one way. On the community EQ-Bench 3 table the non-reasoning Qwen3.5-4B scores **22.6**, about 54% above the 2B (14.7), and even beats the 32B Qwen3-32B (14.5), an older-generation comparison but a sign that the 4B is competent on emotional-intelligence-style judging ([HF table](https://huggingface.co/PrentisAI/hivemind-32b-preview/discussions/1)). Thinking adds only +4.5 at 4B (27.1), so switching it off for voice latency costs less than dropping from 4B to 2B. Artificial Analysis shows the same steep ladder on reasoning (Index 27 for 4B, 16 for 2B, 9 for 0.8B), alongside an **80% hallucination rate** for the 4B, so expect confident small-talk errors about facts ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). On a fixed memory budget a 4-bit 4B should beat an 8-bit 2B: the FP16 gap between Qwen3 1.7B and 4B is about 10 MMLU points against a 2-5 point 4-bit penalty ([arXiv 2505.02214](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02214v1)). That is an inference from MMLU, not from conversation quality.

Counter-evidence exists and you should weigh it. Gemma is repeatedly described as better for "human" writing and more succinct than Qwen ([Reddit, July 2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1uvl312/snapshots/2026-07-17T18%3A53%3A30.39359Z)), and in a role-play thread Gemma 4B was "the most coherent" while Qwen3.5 "sometimes flipped roles" ([Reddit, Sept 2026](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1w6n7zf/snapshots/2026-09-04T21%3A20%3A12.3645Z)). These are single anecdotes, and Gemma 4B drifted off-topic in the same thread. That is why `gemma-3-text-4b-it-4bit` (2.56 GB, `gemma3_text`, a clean text-only load) belongs in your A/B. I would not use the one Qwen3.5-4B companion fine-tune, `wyrdsekai/companion-3.5-4b-mlx` (2.37 GB), as the default, since it has about 25 downloads a month and no evals, but it is cheap to include as a third arm ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/wyrdsekai/companion-3.5-4b-mlx)).

Qwen3.5-4B has two further practical advantages. Its config has only **8 full-attention layers out of 32** with 4 KV heads, giving roughly 32 KB of KV per token (about 128 MB at 4k context) against about 144 KB per token for Qwen3-4B, by my arithmetic from the configs ([Qwen3.5-4B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B/raw/main/config.json), [Qwen3-4B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-4B/raw/main/config.json)). And mlx-swift-lm 3.32.3 has dedicated Qwen3.5 code (`Qwen35.swift`) plus recent decode fixes: compiled decode traces, fused GDN projections and a precision fix ([3.32.3 release](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/releases/tag/3.32.3)). The multimodal-labelled mlx-community checkpoints load text-only because the vision weights are dropped in `sanitize`. The trade-off is weight size: at 3.03 GB it is the largest model that is comfortable on 8 GB. Measured peak memory was 4.13 GB on an M5 Pro, and 1.8 GB of that above the file size is unexplained ([smartypantsnet](https://huggingface.co/datasets/smartypantsnet/mac-benchmarks)). If your Activity Monitor goes yellow, fall back to `Qwen3.5-2B-4bit` or `Qwen3.5-2B-6bit`, not to a 3-bit 4B: 3-bit costs 31-36% perplexity for 10-20% speed ([smartypantsnet](https://huggingface.co/datasets/smartypantsnet/mac-benchmarks)).

Three Qwen3.5 specifics matter for warmth and latency. First, the HF chat template defaults to thinking off and emits an empty `<think></think>` block, but the Swift `ReasoningConfig` declares `defaultOn: true`, so pass `additionalContext: ["enable_thinking": false]` explicitly and confirm the first tokens are not `<think>` ([chat template](https://huggingface.co/mlx-community/Qwen3.5-2B-4bit/raw/main/chat_template.jinja), [QwenReasoningProtocol.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/QwenReasoningProtocol.swift)). Second, use the recommended non-thinking sampling (temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20, presence_penalty 1.5) as a starting point ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B)). Third, the verbosity problem is real in general: a speech-to-speech benchmark found model replies run longer than human ones, with humans averaging about 742 ms response latency ([SPEARBench](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.05365)), so enforce brevity with the system prompt and a `maxTokens` cap of about 100-150.

## Reflex brain: a 1.2B opener, with an honest caveat

The reflex role is narrow: emit a short acknowledgement or first clause within a few hundred milliseconds while the deep brain works. The only direct evidence on small-model chat floors is a developer's LFM2.5 test: a 350M model held a voice for single sentences but lost the thread in casual multi-turn chat, while the **1.2B held the conversation**; automated metrics passed on both, so only hand-talking found the failure ([Ertas blog](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m-vs-350m-vs-1-2b-on-device)). Below about 1B, coherence is therefore risky, which argues against Qwen3.5-0.8B as the default (IFEval 59.9, against 86.2 for LFM2.5-1.2B, and a steep falloff in Qwen3.5 capability from 4B to 0.8B per [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/qwen3-5-small-models)). If the reflex only ever produces two or three words ("mm, yeah, let me think"), a template or a tiny model is enough; if it must keep up with context, 1.2B is the floor.

`LFM2-1.2B-4bit` is the verified, loadable choice: 659 MB, `lfm2` registered in `LLMTypeRegistry`, and listed in `LLMRegistry` as `lfm2_1_2b_4bit` (source read at tag 3.32.3, per the notes). The IFEval and multi-turn evidence is for LFM2.5, not LFM2, and the notes did not check whether an mlx-community LFM2.5-1.2B conversion exists, so search the hub for one and, if found, prefer it. Do not read IFEval as warmth: it checks constraints like length and format, which is loosely useful for "one or two sentences, no bullets" and says nothing about charm. The license is custom (LFM Open License v1.0) but free for personal use and sub-$10M companies. Liquid itself does not recommend the family for knowledge-heavy tasks ([Ertas blog](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m-vs-350m-vs-1-2b-on-device)), which suits an opener but means the reflex must never answer substantive questions. The A/B contenders are `gemma-3-1b-it-qat-4bit` (a QAT build, so quantization loss should be smaller than a post-hoc 4-bit) and `Qwen3.5-0.8B-4bit` (same tokenizer family as the deep brain). A hybrid-architecture caveat: LFM2 mixes convolution and attention blocks, and its speed claims are vendor CPU numbers, with no MLX data found ([Liquid AI](https://www.liquid.ai/en/blog/liquid-foundation-models-v2-our-second-series-of-generative-ai-models)), so measure it rather than trusting 2x-faster claims.

## Latency levers: caching first, speculation mostly off the table

**Prompt caching is the big win.** First-token latency is compute-bound and later tokens are bandwidth-bound ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/exploring-llms-mlx-m5)). At the measured 330 tok/s prefill, a 500-token system prompt costs about **1.5 s** on the 4B and about 0.8 s on the 2B at 651 tok/s, which is TTFT you pay every turn if the cache is rebuilt (my arithmetic from the measured rates; the notes' ~1-2 s estimate is looser). `ChatSession` reuses the KV cache across turns automatically when the new rendered prompt strictly extends the cached tokens; otherwise it rebuilds. You can also warm the static prefix at launch with a one-token generation, or persist it with `ChatSession.saveCache(to:)` and restore it via `ChatSession(_:promptCache:)`, copying caches if you build several sessions ([ChatSession.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/ChatSession.swift), [PromptCacheReusePolicy.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/PromptCacheReusePolicy.swift)). One risk is specific to your pick: Qwen3.5's recurrent GatedDeltaNet caches are **not trimmable**, so the rewind-to-common-prefix rule cannot fire, and a template that re-renders earlier assistant turns differently (the template only keeps `<think>` blocks after the last user query) could force a full rebuild each turn. This is inferred, not tested; verify by logging `promptTokenCount` and `promptTime` from `GenerateCompletionInfo` on turns 2-5. If it rebuilds, keep the history rendering byte-stable or move to the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 arm, whose plain attention cache supports trimming.

**Speculative decoding is the wrong tool for Qwen3.5.** The classic path (`SpeculativeDecodingConfig(draftModel:numDraftTokens: 5)`) throws "Speculative decoding requires trimmable KV caches" for any hybrid or recurrent cache, so Qwen3.5 cannot be target or draft ([Evaluate.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/Evaluate.swift)). Qwen3.5's native MTP route exists (`Qwen3.5-4B-MTP-4bit`, 5-bit and bf16 checkpoints are on mlx-community), but it is restricted to greedy sampling, has no hook in `ChatSession`, and no 0.8B or 2B MTP checkpoint appeared in the notes' search, so it conflicts with the varied, warm sampling you want ([MTPDrafterModel.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/MTPDrafterModel.swift), [HF search](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=mlx-community&search=MTP&limit=50)). Speculation also raises decode throughput only, not TTFT, and a Qwen3-0.6B-4bit draft costs another ~0.68 GB. If the A/B favours the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 arm, the draft pairing `Qwen3-0.6B-4bit` becomes valid, gated with `memoryPolicy: .recommendedWorkingSet`; acceptance rates and speed-ups on 8 GB are unmeasured. For short spoken replies of 20-40 tokens, the cost-benefit is weak, so a small, fast reflex plus cached prefix will do more.

**KV-cache quantization and `maxKVSize` are not worth enabling by default.** Both apply only to attention layers (`kvBits`, `kvGroupSize` default 64, `quantizedKVStart`, `kvScheme` such as `"affine8"`), and on Qwen3.5 those are only 8 of 32 layers while the recurrent state stays full precision, so on a 4k-token voice context the saving is tens of MB ([Evaluate.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXLMCommon/Evaluate.swift), `KVCacheTree.swift`). `maxKVSize` also makes full-attention layers rotate, which overwrites everything but the first four tokens and breaks exact prefix reuse once it wraps, so if used it must exceed system prompt plus conversation. Turn them on only if Activity Monitor shows pressure; their quality effect on hybrid layers is unmeasured. Better memory discipline is to set `Memory.cacheLimit` explicitly (64-256 MB; the library's own Foundation Models bridge uses 256 MB for a 3B model) and never raise the wired limit on 8 GB, because wired-memory exhaustion has caused kernel panics on larger Macs ([mlx-lm issue 883](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/issues/883), [MLXLanguageModel.swift](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/blob/3.32.3/Libraries/MLXFoundationModels/MLXLanguageModel.swift)). Two further cheap points: use group-size-32 or DWQ 4-bit builds if you want a small quality edge (about 9% slower in one run), and budget for TTS contention: MLX serialises Metal work per process, so STT, LLM and TTS take turns, and you should pipeline sentence by sentence and consider running TTS in a separate process ([instagit summary](https://instagit.com/huggingface/speech-to-speech/mlx-lock-contention-apple-silicon-num-pipelines/)). No measurement of that slowdown exists.

## Memory plan on 8 GB

Budget roughly 2.5-3.5 GB for macOS and background apps and about 5-6 GB for model plus context; this split is a third-party estimate, not an Apple figure ([bestllmfor](https://bestllmfor.com/guide/llm-macbook-air-m3/)). Weights alone give 3.03 GB (4B) + 0.66 GB (reflex) = about 3.7 GB, before KV (small), the Kokoro TTS (82M parameters) and STT, so the total plausibly lands at 4.5-5.5 GB with buffers. That is feasible but tight, and unmeasured. Two mitigations: load the deep brain lazily (a 1.8x faster parallel weight loader landed in 3.32.3, per the [release notes](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-swift-lm/releases/tag/3.32.3)) so the reflex is up in under a second, and read `GPU.maxRecommendedWorkingSetBytes()` at startup to see your real cap, which no source gave for an 8 GB M3. Memory pressure turning yellow or tok/s collapsing mid-conversation means swapping; the fix is the 2B fallback, not a larger wired limit.

## A/B test plan to run on your Mac

Measure first, judge second. Log `promptTime`, `generateTime`, `promptTokenCount`, `generationTokenCount` from `GenerateCompletionInfo`, and `Memory.peakMemory` per turn, with Activity Monitor memory pressure open. Use one fixed persona prompt of about 300-500 tokens, one fixed 20-turn script of casual chat (greetings, venting, jokes, a vague question, a topic change, a correction, a request to be brief), temperature fixed per arm, and no thinking. Blind the outputs: have a script dump all arms' replies to a file with shuffled labels and score them yourself, since automated metrics passed a model that failed a human test ([Ertas blog](https://www.ertas.ai/blog/lfm2-5-230m-vs-350m-vs-1-2b-on-device)).

| Step | What to run | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Smoke and memory | Load each deep arm alone, then with the reflex and TTS running; 20 turns | No yellow memory pressure; stable tok/s from turn 1 to 20; note peak memory |
| 2. Deep-brain quality | Arms: `Qwen3.5-4B-4bit`, `gemma-3-text-4b-it-4bit`, `Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507-4bit`, `Qwen3.5-2B-6bit`, optionally the companion fine-tune. Blind-rate warmth, naturalness, brevity (1-3 sentences), persona consistency 1-5 | Winner beats runner-up by clear margin on warmth and brevity, not just on facts |
| 3. Reflex quality | Arms: `LFM2-1.2B-4bit` (and LFM2.5-1.2B if found), `gemma-3-1b-it-qat-4bit`, `Qwen3.5-0.8B-4bit`; 30 openers across moods; also check multi-turn drift over 10 turns | Openers fit mood, no repetition, no factual claims, no role flips |
| 4. Latency | Per arm: TTFT and decode with a cold vs cached system prompt; turns 2-5 with `ChatSession` | Cached TTFT under ~0.4 s on the deep brain; `promptTokenCount` on turn 3+ near new-tokens-only, else cache is rebuilding |
| 5. Quant check | Deep winner at 4-bit vs 4-bit g32 vs 6-bit | Keep 4-bit unless 6-bit is clearly warmer and memory stays green |
| 6. Concurrency | Run reflex + deep + TTS during a reply; compare decode tok/s with TTS on and off | Decode drop under about 25%; otherwise move TTS to a separate process or chunk by sentence |
| 7. Optional speculation | Only if the Qwen3-4B-Instruct arm wins: add `Qwen3-0.6B-4bit` draft; read `acceptedDraftTokens` / `proposedDraftTokens` | Net speed-up on 20-40 token replies; otherwise drop it |

Decide as follows: if Qwen3.5-4B wins quality and its cached TTFT is healthy, ship it. If its cache keeps rebuilding or memory goes yellow, ship the Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 arm (or Qwen3.5-2B). If Gemma 3 4B wins on warmth by a visible margin, accept the lack of EQ-Bench evidence and take it, since that single test is the one that tracks what you care about.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which model is warmest" to "which model can you afford to find out about." No source ranks sub-4B models on warmth, persona fidelity or sycophancy, so the choice rests on proxies: the EQ-Bench 3 gap that favours Qwen3.5-4B, a floor around 1.2B for multi-turn coherence, and anecdotes favouring Gemma for human-sounding text. The clearest actionable finding is architectural: Qwen3.5's hybrid design buys you a tiny KV cache but costs you classic speculative decoding and probably trimmable-cache reuse, which makes prefix prompt caching and brevity prompting your main latency tools rather than draft models or KV quantization.

The second implication is that the safest design hedges rather than bets: both brains are interchangeable behind one protocol, the deep brain has a 2B fallback with the same tokenizer family and chat template, and the reflex is constrained to short, non-factual openers so its weakest trait cannot surface. Run the A/B before committing, and rerun it when new sub-4B releases arrive, since Qwen's small-model line appears to have stalled at 3.5 and Gemma 4's efficient models are too large for this machine.
